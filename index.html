Food & culture

Long before anyone built a launch pad here, this was Acadiana — and that culture isn't going anywhere. Our own crawfish went to space before the rockets did.

  • The boil

    Crawfish season runs roughly late winter through early summer, and a backyard boil — potatoes, corn, sausage, and enough cayenne to make a point — is still the region's real town hall.

  • Cajun & Creole

    Two distinct food traditions that get lumped together: Cajun cooking grew out of rural Acadiana, Creole out of New Orleans' city kitchens. Both show up on a good boudin or gumbo, for different reasons.

  • Zydeco & Cajun music

    Accordion, fiddle, and a rubboard, sung mostly in French — this is still living, working music, played at dance halls across the parish most weekends.

  • Mardi Gras, Acadiana style

    Out here it's less parade, more countryside Courir de Mardi Gras — costumed riders chasing chickens through the fields for the community gumbo pot.

  • The boudin trail

    Gas-station boudin is a genuine local pastime and a point of fierce, friendly pride — everyone has an opinion about whose is best.

  • Festival season

    From crawfish festivals to rice festivals to Mardi Gras runs, Acadiana's calendar is built around gathering. We'll post specific dates here as they're confirmed.

Gumbo season

The pots are coming out again. A couple of gumbo landmarks worth knowing — from a Lafayette dining room to a wide-open cookoff in New Iberia.
